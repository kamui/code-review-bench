# Rationale for the bucket proposal

Recommend two reference bands and seven claim outcomes. Add minor defect to claims. Merge advisory and inconsequential into suggestion or observation. Keep minor defects outside recall, with no penalty for silence.

I considered a third reference band, splitting other-material, admitting every true bug, bonus credit for minor findings, renaming advice, and merging all rejected claims. Each either changes what silence costs, rewards extra comments, or hides an evidence distinction the scorecard needs. A name-only change would leave the contradictory older-fault rule and uncertain benefit threshold intact.

The deciding cases are ripgrep's harmless startup error, requests' private cipher customization, and tRPC's two older compile failures. They require three different explanations: a supported minor defect, an accurate warning about unsupported use, and material problems exposed by the change. They do not require three scored impact bands.

The audit supports removing the advisory/inconsequential benefit distinction. It does not prove that a minor-defect category will be reliable. Calibrate that boundary before paying for the full regrade. The strongest objection is that the new category may add disagreement without changing detection scores; if that happens, keep defect status as an evidence fact within optional feedback instead.

Preserve saved decisions unless the owner expressly changes them. In particular, first-round ruling 31 initially chose serious for truststore, but the later band-check receipt changed GT-i6 to other-material. The proposal follows the later decision and identifies second-pass changes that the current reference file has not yet incorporated.
