**Scoring methodology assessment, 29 September 2026**

Keep the twelve PRs and the current separation of detection, false findings, and fix quality. The benchmark has a sound foundation for comparing practical review configurations. Its main weaknesses are the consistency of adjudication, the narrow range of accepted problems, and the confidence implied by an aggregate ranking. I would audit the saved judgments and improve the reporting before spending on more repetitions.

This assessment uses the current registry at repository revision `4d45101c5ccf14295a41e59f0ebda61c2e25e512`. It changes no grades or benchmark evidence. The registry contains twelve PR tasks, nine with fourteen reference problems and three with no established eligible problem, thirteen configurations, and 411 attempts. References and grades remain model-assisted. The human-authority and release policies describe the intended official process; they do not establish that the imported data has received human approval. See [README](../../../README.md), [human authority](../../adr/0002-human-authority-for-new-and-disputed-findings.md), and [reference releases](../../adr/0003-version-reference-findings.md).

The explorer's current trial metric and the preserved historical metric answer different questions. The current metric averages repetitions within each buggy PR, then gives each buggy PR equal weight. A terminal valid attempt supplies the trial's findings, while the costs of its infrastructure predecessors remain included. An unadmitted terminal attempt earns zero detection; an admitted review can retain recovery credit despite mapping-level incomplete coverage. Unrun or pending trials remain missing. Historical scoring includes failed predecessor attempts in its detection denominator. Keep both definitions explicit and preserve their earlier results. [Current implementation](../../../src/lib/metrics.ts), [historical implementation](../../../bench/tools/score.py), and [design](../../v1-design.md) document these differences.

Separating detection from remedies is correct. Identifying a real problem remains valuable when the proposed repair is absent or incomplete. Separating detection from false findings is also useful because the reader can inspect the tradeoff. A combined score would require choosing how many correct findings compensate for one false claim, then hide that choice inside the ranking. I would continue to present several measurements rather than add a general star rating, F1 score, or severity-weighted total.

The macro score is defensible, but it measures performance on the typical selected buggy PR rather than the fraction of every reference problem recovered. A reference in a one-problem PR has three times the weight of a reference in Requests or tRPC. One additional recovery in one of three repetitions moves the full score by 3.70 percentage points on a one-problem task and 1.23 points on a three-problem task. Show micro detection as a sensitivity view and keep the task and reference counts beside every filtered comparison.

Sol and Astra illustrate why the mean needs task-level context. Both score 69.75%. Sol recovers ripgrep's reference in all three repetitions, while Astra recovers it in none. Astra does better on Base UI and SeaweedFS. Removing ripgrep gives Astra a 12.5-point lead; removing SeaweedFS gives Sol an 8.33-point lead. These are exact sensitivities of this corpus, not confidence intervals for unseen PRs. The setup that fits the user's work depends partly on which kinds of problems recur there.

Their average costs are approximately 13.7 and 2.2 times Luna's. These results are useful configuration comparisons, but the skills ran through Codex 0.159.0 `exec` while the built-in used 0.158.0 native review. Historical comparisons also differ in sandboxing, prompts, and test permissions. Hold those settings fixed in future experiments before attributing an improvement specifically to the skill. The strongest improvement to the grading rubric is to make truth, scope, materiality, and support separate decisions. The current rubric uses `defect`, `false-finding`, `non-material`, and `unresolved`. It says an unsupported assertion can be false, while the grader also must cite what refutes a false finding. That leaves room for different interpretations of “not demonstrated.” Preserve this historical vocabulary, but make the following distinctions explicit in a future rubric.

| Evidence and relevance | Proposed treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Supported mechanism, realistic trigger, and material consequence caused or worsened by this change | Actionable finding; match an existing reference or queue a novel claim for official adjudication. |
| Accurate advice with a concrete benefit that does not justify blocking this change | Useful advisory feedback, measured separately from detection. |
| Accurate observation with little demonstrated benefit | Non-material observation or clutter, distinct from useful advice. |
| Evidence contradicts the claimed problem or consequence | Refuted false finding, with the counterevidence recorded. |
| A defect assertion still lacks necessary support after an adequate check | Unsupported assertion; identify the missing support and keep its reliability cost visible. |
| Available evidence or execution restrictions prevent a fair decision | Unresolved; state what would settle it. |
| Accurate issue is preexisting or outside the review contract | Scope exclusion; do not call it factually false merely because it earns no detection credit. |

An actionable finding should identify enough of the trigger, mechanism, violated behavior or obligation, and consequence for a maintainer to investigate or correct it. It need not contain a patch or describe every manifestation. A reproduction is strong evidence, but source reasoning, a documented contract, or a valid static counterexample can also establish a problem. Conversely, rarity alone should not disqualify a reachable concurrency failure. Judge persistence, recovery, supported deployment conditions, and impact. A short race window can still cause lasting data loss.

The Base UI grades show why truth and defect validity must remain separate. Sol's three false findings describe a controlled value being committed by the consumer and then validated by the prop-sync effect. The code behavior is real. The grade rejects the claim because validation of the value actually displayed is intended by the PR's controlled-value design. Calling a true code fact a defect can therefore be false. This is a defensible rationale; the wording “no demonstrated failure” in a register is less precise than the explanation in the [Sol scorecard](../../../bench/runs/2026-09-29-codex-sol-high-writable/scoring/r-base-ui-5460/scorecard.v1.md).

There are also concrete consistency questions. Ripgrep's missing completion-initialization ordering is non-material in the [baseline scorecard](../../../bench/runs/2026-09-24-builtin-baseline/scoring/n-ripgrep-2957/scorecard.v1.md), but a reproduced unresolved candidate in the [Sol scorecard](../../../bench/runs/2026-09-29-codex-sol-high-writable/scoring/n-ripgrep-2957/scorecard.v1.md). SeaweedFS's concurrent `UpdateEntry` de-indexing claim is unresolved in the [Sol scorecard](../../../bench/runs/2026-09-29-codex-sol-high-writable/scoring/s-seaweedfs-10735/scorecard.v1.md) and non-material in the [Thermo scorecard](../../../bench/runs/2026-09-29-codex-thermo-high/scoring/s-seaweedfs-10735/scorecard.v1.md). Their precise trigger assumptions and patch attribution need comparison before deciding whether the judgments conflict. They should share an adjudication case, rather than receive independent final threshold decisions merely because they came from different runs.

Build that shared queue around canonical claims per pinned PR revision. Group repeated manifestations, preserve every review's wording and qualifiers, and record the evidence, decision, reason, authority, and reference version. Have the human adjudicator resolve new and consequential disputed cases once, then apply the decision consistently to every comparable saved review. Prioritize unresolved claims, claims that materially alter a comparison, rare but severe failures, and ambiguous testing obligations. Follow with an audit of all fourteen references and a stratified sample of accepted, rejected, and non-material items. Include negative judgments in the sample, since checking only accepted problems cannot reveal valid findings dismissed by the grader.

This is calibration of the judge, not proof that the existing graders are biased. The benchmark's uniform rendering and hidden identities are helpful controls. The baseline graded several configurations together per PR, while later runs used separate grading sessions for each run and PR. Record that difference and test cross-run consistency on shared claim families. Research has demonstrated position, verbosity, and self-preference effects in LLM evaluation, which makes a human-checked calibration sample and a second judge family useful safeguards. Disagreements should trigger evidence review; agreement between models should not substitute for the required human authority. [Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685), [Panickssery et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076).

Mixed findings need more precise treatment. Thermo's Hono `att-020` receives GT-p1 credit and a sufficient-fix grade even though the scorecard explicitly identifies an incorrect side claim about the form validator. Preserve detection of the correct multipart-cache problem, but record the independently false assertion too. Otherwise, packaging a wrong claim beside a true one can erase its reliability cost. Split independently checkable assertions during normalization while deduplicating detection at the reference-problem level. Do not split ordinary explanation into artificial extra findings. [Hono scorecard](../../../bench/runs/2026-09-29-codex-thermo-high/scoring/p-hono-5067/scorecard.v1.md).

Defect grouping also needs a consistent rule. Requests groups shared certificate mutation and custom-adapter override behavior under GT-i1; tRPC has three references around related `Overwrite` behavior. The grouping may be defensible, but it affects both the denominator and the meaning of a “partial” fix. Record independently violated outcomes and their manifestations, and state why they belong to one problem or several. Shared code location or one upstream patch should not decide the grouping. Preserve primary IDs across releases and assess material regrouping as an explicit sensitivity.

The answer key has already grown through discoveries, including Requests GT-i3, tRPC GT-j2/GT-j3, and ripgrep GT-n1. That is evidence that references are incomplete, not grounds to delete those well-supported discoveries. Keep a reference release frozen while new candidates await adjudication. On the next human-approved release, update denominators and regrade all comparable retained outputs, including earlier outputs and outputs from competing configurations. Publish accepted discoveries separately while they await that release. This avoids making the discovering setup's wording or proposed patch the exclusive route to credit. See the [versioned registers](../../../bench/targets/) and [release policy](../../adr/0003-version-reference-findings.md).

A low false-finding count currently leaves two important questions unanswered. First, Sol's eight unresolved items represent four candidate families, including two ripgrep claims repeated three times. They have no detection credit and no false-finding penalty. Display unresolved workload and adjudication coverage prominently, so tentative results cannot imply established trustworthiness. Second, Fable has 247 non-material items against Sol's five over the same 36 trials. Some may be useful advice. The count does establish a large difference in reading work that the false-findings axis misses.

Add false claims per admitted review alongside the current per-trial count, the fraction of clean reviews containing a false claim, non-material items, duplicates, and unresolved claims. Current per-trial false findings can decrease when a trial fails, because unadmitted terminal outputs contribute zero false claims but remain in the denominator. Keep completion beside these figures and retain invalid-output claims in an audit subtotal. If later adding precision, define its claim unit, show how many claims remain unjudged, and do not count useful nonblocking advice as automatically false. Similarly, expose zero-recovery and approve-on-buggy outcomes without assuming every method uses the same verdict vocabulary. [Export admission](../../../tools/export_explorer.py) and [review-level grading](../../../bench/rubric/scoring.v1.md) contain the relevant rules.

Keep fix quality separate. For future remedy assessment, distinguish absent, partial, sufficient, incorrect, harmful, and unverified advice. The current “partial” category cannot communicate the difference between addressing one manifestation and introducing another bug. Record whether sufficiency comes from inspection, a focused check, or an executed repair. A simple imperative such as removing a stray prompt marker can be a sufficient suggestion without a separate Fix section. For complex repairs, show both quality among detected problems and sufficient-outcome recall over all eligible problems. Aggregate fix-quality ranking remains deferred under the existing design.

Failure handling needs a prospective cause policy. Distinguish demonstrated benchmark faults, infrastructure interruptions, reviewer policy violations, and failures to complete the required output. A normalizer stop alone does not establish a benchmark fault. For example, the preserved [failure follow-up](../../failure-followup.md) documents missing artifacts after sandbox denials in earlier skill runs. Replay saved outputs only when a parser or audit defect is demonstrated, and preserve the original interpretation. The [Thermo exclusion receipt](../../../bench/runs/2026-09-29-codex-thermo-high/supplemental-exclusions.v1.json) records an explicit instruction to retain the original cohort and exclude supplemental retries. This analysis gives no reason to override that recorded selection. Report a separately labelled diagnostic interpretation if later investigation warrants one.

For uncertainty, distinguish randomness on this fixed corpus from sensitivity to the chosen PRs and uncertainty in the grades. Fresh sessions prevent conversation carryover; repeated reviews of one PR do not create new PR identities. Show per-task differences, replicate variability, and leave-one-task-out results now. Within-task resampling can explore repeatability conditional on the saved grades. Paired task-cluster resampling can explore task-mix sensitivity, but nine purposively selected buggy PRs cannot establish representative population coverage. State those assumptions, rather than attach a confidence label to every bootstrap interval. Do not use “less than one PR's weight” as a significance test. The methodological recommendations follow work on [multiple-dataset comparisons](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v7/demsar06a.html) and [clustered resampling](https://rjournal.github.io/articles/RJ-2023-015/); their application to this benchmark is an inference.

The coverage gap is consequential for rating skills. The proposed profile labels on current references contain Functional, Reliability, Security, Performance, and Testing, but none for Architecture, Maintainability, or Scalability. All fourteen severities are unclassified, and the exporter currently hardcodes null severity. Publishing severity comparisons will require approved labels and an extractor that reads them. A maintainability-focused skill can identify useful structural concerns and still receive little detection credit. Keep the chosen PRs, but add future positives with concrete architectural obligations, measurable maintenance costs, realistic scalability failures, and more clean security/concurrency controls. Include correct counterexamples that invite plausible objections. Test-quality findings need a common materiality standard: GraphQL's accepted lost-branch-coverage problem establishes that product runtime failures are not the only eligible defects. [Profiles](../../../bench/profiles.json), [GraphQL reference](../../../bench/targets/k-graphql-js-1582/register.v1.json), and [severity extraction](../../../tools/export_explorer.py) substantiate these gaps.

Useful advice can also improve understanding or offer a better solution without justifying a blocking finding. An empirical study of modern code review found benefits beyond defect detection. That supports an advisory-usefulness evaluation alongside detection, rather than awarding every cleanup suggestion a reference point. A small blinded maintainer audit can assess whether advice is correct, specific, useful for this change, and worth its implementation and reading cost. Treat that as a separate outcome until it has calibrated examples. [Bacchelli and Bird](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/expectations-outcomes-and-challenges-of-modern-code-review/).

I would sequence the improvements as follows:

1. Audit the saved claim families and references, resolve consequential disputes with human authority, and calibrate materiality with accepted and rejected examples.
2. Publish a versioned decision rubric and explicit admission/failure rules. Expose unresolved claims, review burden, clean false alarms, completion, and task-level sensitivity beside detection.
3. Issue a human-approved reference release and independently assign severity. Regrade retained comparable reviews; preserve older releases. Show Critical/High detection and classification coverage separately from the main score.
4. Add fresh PRs that supply missing positive categories and matched clean controls. Keep them unseen during skill tuning and freeze the selection and judging rules before running reviews.
5. Run matched skill comparisons under the same model, client, effort, execution policy, and declared resource constraints. Use native-setting comparisons for practical setup selection, and separate cost-matched experiments when studying efficiency.
